# fidelity: the honest promise (what each verb can guarantee on each path)

Studio commands adjust **design intent**. Each render path honors that intent as far as its own engine allows.
The intent is the same everywhere; the pixels are not. Promise the intent, name the path's limit honestly.

## The rule

- **Commands are available on every path.** You never tell a creator a verb "does not work" on their doneness.
- **You cannot promise identical results across paths.** You CAN promise **all three meet the floor**, and that
  the **well-done MP4 is pixel-exact**.
- When a path cannot enforce a piece of craft, Studio does the next honest thing: it gives the exact step to
  do it, rather than pretending it was done.

## The matrix (verified against the engine)

| | Hook / thought / takeover / accent text | Line captions |
|---|---|---|
| **🥩 well-done, MP4** (HyperFrames + ffmpeg) | **Exact.** Authored in pixels: size, line-height, position, wrap all guaranteed. | **Exact.** Same. |
| **🥩 medium, CapCut + `.mov`** | **Exact look.** Baked into the overlay's pixels; CapCut cannot override it. Moves as a block, not re-typeable. | **Exact look**, as a block. |
| **🥩 raw, editable CapCut** | **Size + position + wrap honored** (the engine writes CapCut's own native values; there is no re-interpretation). Line-height is honored where the engine sets it (currently thought bubbles; other roles use CapCut's default until wired). | **Native CapCut Auto Captions.** The creator generates these in-app. The engine does not touch them. Studio ADVISES the size + nudge; it does not enforce. |

**The one asterisk, stated plainly:** on the raw path, the line captions are CapCut's native Auto Captions.
That is on purpose, it is what gives the creator CapCut's karaoke build and keyword highlight. Studio keeps
those, and for caption craft on raw it hands over the exact in-app move ("Auto captions, Bold Text popup, nudge
just under the chin, keep them inside the safe band"). It never claims to have sized a caption it cannot reach.
Full caption control lives on the MP4 and medium paths.

## Per-verb, per-path

- **font**, hooks/thoughts/takeovers: controllable on all three paths (size + position everywhere; full
  line-height on MP4/medium, size+position+wrap on raw). Line captions: exact on MP4/medium; on raw, advises
  the in-app caption size + placement.
- **tidy**, placement + safe zone: exact on MP4/medium; on raw, exact for engine text, advisory for the
  native-caption nudge.
- **accent**, the accent shade + legibility over footage: exact on MP4/medium; on raw, exact for engine text
  color; native-caption color/highlight is the creator's in-app choice, Studio advises the readable option.
- **glow**, the final pass reports honestly per path: on MP4 it confirms the pixels; on raw it lists the two
  or three in-app nudges left for the creator, so nothing silently ships wrong.

## Why CapCut exists in the product at all (goes in the core, always)

This is not a fallback used only when fidelity is imperfect. It is the truth about the CapCut path: **CapCut is
the control layer.** It is where the creator adds their own touch on top of what the engine built. Hands-on by
design, not by limitation.

Say it with the same honesty as "you direct it, it is not magic":

> The CapCut path is about customizability. You are not going to get a perfectly queued, ready-to-post video
> from it. If you want the ready-to-post one, that is the well-done MP4. The CapCut path is like having an
> editing assistant who trimmed your footage, thought through your hooks, and loaded your thought bubbles and
> your graphics, everything you need to execute, handed to you on a platter. You just plate the dish.

Studio's job on the CapCut path is to make sure what lands on that platter already clears the floor.
